# E2 — SaaS Bots

**One-liner:** own a bot with a monthly fee — users pay every month, you own the product.

## What it is
A "software as a service" bot: something channel owners / communities need every day, sold as a subscription (€9–29/month). Examples: auto-posting/scheduling bot, channel analytics bot, member-management bot, moderation bot. You build once, users pay monthly. The strongest money shape on this whole list *if* it finds users.

## Money flow
- **Who pays:** channel owners / community admins (they already spend money on their channels).
- **How much:** €9–29/month per user. 100 users = €900–2,900/month. 1,000 = real business.
- **How often:** recurring — the entire point. Churn (users cancelling) is the enemy.

## Pipe test
- **Who owns the pipe:** we own the product and the servers. Telegram is just the platform the bot runs on.
- **Day-400:** the user base + code is ours. This is a *business*, the strongest pipe on the list.

## Build steps
- [ ] Pick ONE pain: what do channel owners complain about? (posting schedule = strongest, most obvious)
- [ ] Build the bot (I do it)
- [ ] Payment: Stripe subscription or Stars
- [ ] Landing: a one-page "what it does, what it costs"
- [ ] Find first 10 users: offer free month, ask for feedback
- [ ] Iterate, then raise the price

## Traffic engine
- First 10 users = hand-picked (you talk to channel owners directly)
- Users tell other admins — admin-to-admin referrals are strong in Telegram
- Posting in bot directories / admin communities

## Hermes sustain role
- Everything technical: servers, uptime, updates, new features
- User support (bot + me)
- Monthly churn analysis: who cancels and why
- Pricing experiments

## Launch speed
1–2 months to first paying users — the slowest of the quick ideas, but the biggest upside.

## Risks
- Nobody pays if the pain isn't real — talk to 10 channel owners BEFORE building (I'll draft the questions)
- Free competitors exist — the moat is doing one thing really well + being first in the user's head
- Churn: users cancel. Keep adding what they ask for.

## First 3 moves
1. I draft the "10 channel owner questions" list.
2. You ask 5 real channel owners what hurts.
3. Build the first version.
